I started building BuzzBuzz in summer 2026. My brother and I had a challenge going: who could get an app into the store first? I won. The app still had to do something. I built and shipped it myself on iOS and Android.

I wanted a step counter that left a little room for company. Most step counters are good at telling you how far you walked. I wanted somewhere to share the walk itself, even if the most interesting thing about it was a dog in a raincoat.

I was thinking about walks people already take together. School runs, the long way home, or twenty minutes with a friend that turns into one more block. The step count can tell you how much you walked. The photo can tell your hive why you stopped.

## How the bees got in

I was working on a bee design for a friend who's a beekeeper. My friend's brand is called Starke Biene. That design got me thinking about bees for BuzzBuzz. I liked that a hive gave the group a name, and that I could make the bee an animated mascot. A step graph would've been harder to put in a tiny hat.

In BuzzBuzz, your group is a [hive](/guides/what-is-a-hive). You can share one photo a day with it using the bee cam. The bee on your profile is yours to dress up. [Meet the bees](/bees/) if you want to see what that last part turned into. I wanted the bee to feel like the friend who asks if you’re going for a walk today. The tiny hat is optional.

## What I left out

I kept hives small and gave the daily photo about a day before it disappears. I left out the big feed of strangers. A photo from an ordinary walk can be worth sending to a friend without needing to live anywhere forever.

I still care about the numbers, like [how many steps are in 5 km](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km) and [how long 10,000 steps takes](/guides/how-long-to-walk-10000-steps). I built BuzzBuzz for the bit after you've worked that out, when you text someone and go outside.
