You don't need to walk at the same time to walk together. Agree on one daily rule, walk on your own local time, and share a photo or step count where the group already looks. Keep it to a few friends and count who showed up each day.

## Pick a rule that survives time zones

Everyone's walking day runs from midnight to midnight where they are. A Monday walk counts for Monday in the walker's time zone, even if the friend reading the message is already eating Tuesday breakfast. Use the date of the walk when you tally check-ins.

Give everyone until their own next morning to post. “Monday walk, posted Tuesday” is enough to make a late photo clear. The extra time is for sending the check-in. If the walk itself happens Tuesday, it counts for Tuesday.

Using one person's clock can make someone else's regular evening walk late every single day. Pin the local-date rule so nobody has to work out the time difference while brushing their teeth.

## Make it fair when fitness differs

Ask what each person can do on a day when work runs late. A friend who walks to work might clear a shared step floor on the way home. A friend who spends the day at a desk might need to set aside time just to catch up.

| Rule | Good when | Watch out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same daily step floor | Friends have similar routines | A desk day puts someone behind from the start |
| Personal target (a little above your usual) | Fitness and schedules vary | Everyone needs to pick an honest baseline |
| Minutes walked | Friends walk at very different paces or have different stride lengths | If you use a step counter, you'll need to time the walk separately |
| Show-up streak (any walk plus a photo) | Friends are busy and scattered | A short loop counts, even if someone else walks for an hour |

If you're considering a shared floor, use [the steps-in-a-mile guide](/guides/how-many-steps-is-1-mile) to picture how much walking it could take. Compare the floor with an ordinary workday before you agree to it. With personal targets, a simple “hit mine” is enough for the check-in.

## Keep the chat alive

Post the view from the walk. A photo of the handwritten sign in a shop window gives the next person something to reply to besides a number. Give the group a loose theme each week if the photos start to feel like homework. Try “your bus stop” and see what waiting for a bus looks like in each city.

Rotate nudge duty each week. If the chat goes quiet, the person on duty can post their own walk and ask who's getting out later. For a missed day, use the [streak guide's advice on what to do when someone misses](/guides/start-a-walking-streak-with-friends#when-someone-misses).

## Try it for two weeks

- **Week 1:** Pin the local-date rule and the next-morning deadline. Any walk plus a photo counts. At the end of the week, ask which days were hardest to fit in.
- **Week 2:** Keep the check-in and add the fairness rule you picked. If showing up is already a stretch, keep that rule and choose a route that's easy to get to on a busy day.

For more on keeping the group small, read [what a hive is](/guides/what-is-a-hive). If your group wants a daily goal, use [the walking streak playbook](/guides/start-a-walking-streak-with-friends).

The photo of the bus stop will probably get more replies than the weekly total.
