10 km is **about 13,000 steps** for many adults, usually **12,500–15,000**. That is more than 10,000 steps. A shorter step means more steps for the same 10 km.

The round sketch is the [5 km figure](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km) doubled. About **1,300 steps per kilometre** times 10 is 13,000. Treat it as a sketch, then check it on a walk you take.

## A step-length chart for 10 km

Divide 10,000 metres by the length of one step. These rows use the same example lengths as the 5 km page. They are examples, not a reading of your walk.

- Shorter step, about 0.67 m: 10,000 ÷ 0.67 = 14,925, about **14,900 steps**
- Middle step, about 0.75 m: 10,000 ÷ 0.75 = 13,333.3, about **13,300 steps**
- Longer step, about 0.80 m: 10,000 ÷ 0.80 = **12,500 steps**

A mile-based cross-check lands in the same neighborhood. The [University of Nebraska Medical Center’s walking guide](https://www.unmc.edu/cfhl/resources/walking.html) puts an average stride at about 2.5 feet and says a mile takes just over 2,000 steps. Using 0.3048 metres in a foot, 2.5 × 0.3048 = 0.762 m, about **0.76 m**. 10 km is about 6.2 miles, twice the 3.1 miles in 5 km. [How many steps are in 1 mile](/guides/how-many-steps-is-1-mile) works that 2.5 foot stride out to 2,112 steps. Then 6.2 × 2,112 = **13,094.4**, about 13,100. Close to the 13,000 sketch, and close to the 0.75 m row.

## 10,000 steps and 10 km

10,000 steps and 10 km are different goals. At a 0.75 m step, 10,000 steps cover 10,000 × 0.75 m = 7,500 m, which is **7.5 km**. [How many miles is 10,000 steps](/guides/how-many-miles-is-10000-steps) puts that count at about **6.5–8 km** for many adults, and 7.5 km sits in that band.

What is left of a 10 km is 10 − 7.5 = **2.5 km**. On the 0.75 m row the full walk is 13,300 steps, so you are 13,300 − 10,000 = **3,300 steps** short. On the round 13,000 figure the gap is about **3,000 steps**. A 10,000-step day is usually about 2.5 km and about 3,000 steps short of a 10 km.

## How long 10 km takes

Pace sets the clock. The [NHS describes a brisk walk as about 3 miles an hour](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/walking-for-health/), faster than a stroll, and you can still talk. 10 km is about 6.2 miles, so 6.2 ÷ 3 × 60 = **124 minutes**, which is **2 hours 4 minutes**. A brisk 10 km takes **about 2 hours**. That pace is about **4.8 km/h**, since 3 miles is 3 ÷ 6.2 × 10 = 4.84 km, rounded to 4.8.

A slower pace takes longer. At 4 km/h, 10 ÷ 4 = **2.5 hours**. A comfortable walk often falls in the **2 to 2.5 hour** span between the brisk pace and that slower one. The span is an estimate from the pace math, not a timed loop. The [5 km guide](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km) puts a comfortable 5 km at often 60–75 minutes. At the same pace, 60 × 2 = 120 minutes and 75 × 2 = 150 minutes, the same 2 to 2.5 hours. Stops and crossings add minutes on the clock. They do not add steps.

[Walking 10,000 steps usually takes about 90–120 minutes](/guides/how-long-to-walk-10000-steps) of actual walking. The chart above puts a 10 km past that step count. If you are counting in half hours, a [30-minute walk is about 3,000 steps](/guides/how-many-steps-is-a-30-minute-walk) at a steady pace.

## You can split the 10 km

The 10 km does not have to be one loop. Four steady half hours are 4 × 3,000 = **12,000 steps**. That is close to 13,000, and ordinary walking between the loops can cover a gap that small. On that same page, an easy stroll is about 2,400 steps in 30 minutes. Four of those are 4 × 2,400 = **9,600 steps**. Against the round 13,000, that is 13,000 − 9,600 = **3,400 steps** still to walk, so the pace matters.

:::bee
Five people can split a 10 km into 2 km each. The group still covers the distance. Your counter only has to record your share.
:::

## Find your own 10 km

If you already counted a 5 km, double that step total. If you know your steps for 1 km, multiply by 10. The [5 km guide](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km) is where that personal number comes from. Or note the counter at the start and end of a route you know is about 10 km, then subtract. Your own number beats the chart.

On a steep or rocky trail, shorter steps can raise the count for the same distance. [A hike still counts](/guides/does-a-hike-count-toward-your-step-goal), and a trail 10 km may take more steps than the pavement rows above.

Walk the 10 km with a few friends this weekend, or give each person a piece of it. [Start a walking streak with friends](/guides/start-a-walking-streak-with-friends) if you want that group out again next week.
