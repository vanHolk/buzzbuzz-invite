10 km is **about 13,000 steps** for many adults, usually **12,500–15,000**. That is more than 10,000 steps. A shorter step means more steps for the same 10 km.

The round sketch is the [5 km figure](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km) doubled. About **1,300 steps per kilometre** times 10 is 13,000. Treat it as a sketch, then check it on a walk you take.

A step counter counts footfalls, not metres. The same 10 km can land higher or lower for ordinary reasons: a shorter step, a pace that stretches each step a little, hills and crossings, or a phone in a bag and a watch on a wrist. Two people can share the route and finish with different totals.

## A step-length chart for 10 km

10,000 metres ÷ your step length. These rows use the same example lengths as the 5 km page. They are examples, not a reading of your walk.

- About 0.67 m: roughly **14,900 steps**
- About 0.75 m: roughly **13,300 steps**
- About 0.80 m: roughly **12,500 steps**

The [University of Nebraska Medical Center’s walking guide](https://www.unmc.edu/cfhl/resources/walking.html) puts an average stride at about 2.5 feet, which works out to just over 2,100 steps a mile, and 10 km is about 6.2 miles, which lands near **13,100**. [How many steps are in 1 mile](/guides/how-many-steps-is-1-mile) has the method.

## 10,000 steps and 10 km

10,000 on the watch is a tidy round number. At a 0.75 m step it covers about **7.5 km**. [How many miles is 10,000 steps](/guides/how-many-miles-is-10000-steps) puts that count at about **6.5–8 km** for many adults, and 7.5 km sits in the middle of the band.

A 10 km still wants about **2.5 km** more, and about **3,000 steps** more. Cheer the 10,000 if you want, and keep walking.

## How long 10 km takes

Pace sets the clock. The [NHS describes a brisk walk as about 3 miles an hour](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/walking-for-health/), faster than a stroll, and you can still talk. That pace is about **4.8 km/h**, so 10 km takes **about 2 hours** of brisk walking.

A comfortable pace is often **2 to 2.5 hours**. That span is an estimate, not a timed loop. Stops and crossings add minutes on the clock. They do not add steps.

[Walking 10,000 steps usually takes about 90–120 minutes](/guides/how-long-to-walk-10000-steps) of actual walking. The chart above puts a 10 km past that step count. Counting in half hours instead, a [30-minute walk is about 3,000 steps](/guides/how-many-steps-is-a-30-minute-walk) at a steady pace.

## You can split the 10 km

The 10 km does not have to be one loop. Four steady 30-minute walks come to about **12,000 steps**, close to 13,000, and normal moving around between them can cover a gap that small. A morning loop, the long way home, and an evening wander are allowed to team up. An easy stroll in those same half hours is closer to 2,400 steps, so the pace still matters.

:::bee
Five people can split a 10 km into 2 km each. The group still covers the distance. Your counter only has to record your share.
:::

## Find your own 10 km

If you already counted a 5 km, double that step total. If you know your steps for 1 km, multiply by 10. The [5 km guide](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km) is where that personal number comes from. Or note the counter at the start and end of a route you know is about 10 km, then subtract. Use that gap next time, instead of a stranger’s average. Your own number beats the chart.

On a steep or rocky trail, shorter steps can raise the count for the same distance. [A hike still counts](/guides/does-a-hike-count-toward-your-step-goal), and a trail 10 km may take more steps than the pavement rows above.

Walk a 10 km with a few friends on a weekend, or give each person a piece of it. [Start a walking streak with friends](/guides/start-a-walking-streak-with-friends) if you want the same group out again.
