Pick a small group. Pick a daily step number you can actually hit. Walk for seven days. Tell each other when you have done it. That is the whole sport.

The streak matters more than the size of the number. A shared 6,000 that everyone reaches will beat a lonely 10,000 that collapses on Wednesday.

## Set it up before the group chat gets bored

1. **Invite people who already like you.** Three to eight is a sweet spot. Big enough that someone is always walking. Small enough that silence is noticeable.
2. **Choose a floor, not a fantasy.** 5,000–8,000 steps is a strong first week for a mixed group. You can raise it later. If you want a distance to picture, [1 mile is about 2,000–2,500 steps](/guides/how-many-steps-is-1-mile) and [5 km is roughly 6,500 steps](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km) for many adults — estimates, not rules.
3. **Agree on the check-in.** A photo from the walk, a step count, or a single “done.” One proof a day. Not a diary.
4. **Name the group.** Silly names survive longer than “Fitness Challenge 2.”
5. **Start tomorrow morning.** Starting “sometime this week” is how streaks move to a farm upstate.

Write the rule in one sentence and pin it. “7,000 steps a day, photo in the chat, seven days, no speeches.” If the rule needs a paragraph, it is already too heavy.

:::bee
A streak with friends is a promise you do not want to break in the group chat. Use that power gently. Notice a missed day. Do not turn it into homework.
:::

## What a good day looks like

You do not need a scenic route or a matching outfit. You need a walk long enough to hit the number, and one small thing worth sending.

The photo is the part people actually open. A bakery window, a weird cloud, a dog in a coat. The step count can ride along underneath. Steps are the score. The picture is the story.

If you use BuzzBuzz, this is the shape of a hive: a few friends, a daily step count, and one photo that does not have to live forever. That is also [why the app is full of bees](/guides/why-we-made-a-step-counter-with-bees). Any group chat you already have will also do. The walking is the point.

## When someone misses

Someone will miss. Decide this on day one.

A **personal streak** is your own days in a row. The group still cheers, but one quiet Tuesday does not end the week for everyone. This is the kinder first version.

A **shared streak** only survives if everybody hits the number. It is fun once people trust the habit. It is a bad way to start with a group that has kids, shifts, or weather.

If a day dies, restart without a speech. The point is the next walk.

## After the seven days

Look at who is still texting. Keep the same floor for another week, or nudge it up by a thousand steps. Do not invent a new sport.

When the evenings get dark, the same group can try the [10,000-step Halloween challenge](/guides/10000-step-halloween-challenge): most of the steps whenever you like, and one walk after sunset. Bring a friend. Bring a jacket.

And if 10,000 ever comes up as the “real” goal, remember it is usually [about 90–120 minutes of actual walking](/guides/how-long-to-walk-10000-steps), not a health requirement and not a personality test. Split it. Send the photo. Go again tomorrow.
