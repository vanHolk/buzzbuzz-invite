Pick a color, go for a daytime walk, and photograph every leaf, door, or bike that matches. Do it with a friend or two and compare when you get back.

One neighborhood loop is the whole route. Photograph from the sidewalk or the path, skip private yards, and leave every leaf where it fell. A block with three street trees can still finish the list. Doors, bikes, windows, and shadows are on it too.

Pick the color before you leave. Say it in the group chat, or choose it on the first corner if you are alone. A common color makes the leaves easy. A strange one puts the work on doors and bikes. You are done when you have walked and sent a photo. Twelve is the full list. A shorter set still counts.

## Twelve things to photograph

Take them in any order. One photo each. If a prompt is missing on your route, walk one more block or let it go.

1. One red leaf, on the twig or already on the ground.
2. One yellow leaf.
3. A leaf that is still half green.
4. A tree that changed before the ones beside it.
5. A pile nobody has raked, seen from the sidewalk.
6. Something orange that is not a pumpkin. A cone, a door, a construction sign.
7. Leaves reflected in a puddle or a window.
8. The same tree from across the street, then from a few steps away.
9. A color you did not expect once the green started to drop.
10. A sharp shadow on a sunny patch of pavement.
11. A door, a bike, or a mailbox in the color you picked.
12. One leaf that still looks like late summer.

Send the whole set, or send the three you like. A gray afternoon still counts. So does a windy one that has already put leaves on the pavement.

## One color before dinner

Two to six friends. Use one walk for the full list, or assign one color a day for a week. Write the rule in one sentence and pin it. “One color, one photo each, posted before dinner.” The first person to find that color in an odd place gets bragging rights. A yellow hydrant counts. So does a blue mailbox, if blue was the color.

In BuzzBuzz this is a Color Hunt: a color prompt, a walk, a photo for your hive. Any group chat can play.

If the photos are still arriving on day eight, keep the people and drop the theme. [A walking streak with friends](/guides/start-a-walking-streak-with-friends) keeps that check-in going after the color week.

If you are walking alone, the compare step can wait. Save the photos and send three later, or keep them. The prompts still give an ordinary loop a job.

## Why the green goes away

[The U.S. Forest Service](https://www.fs.usda.gov/visit/fall-colors/joy-and-science) ties the change to night length: as nights get longer, chlorophyll production slows and is eventually destroyed, and the carotenoids and anthocyanin already in the leaf show through. Carotenoids produce the yellows, oranges, and browns. Anthocyanin produces the reds. The same page lists red maple as brilliant scarlet, sugar maple as orange-red, and aspen as golden yellow.

## When your street turns

[Fall color depends on location](https://www.fs.usda.gov/visit/fall-colors). The Forest Service goes region by region: aspens, oaks, maples, cottonwoods, and golden larches. Elevation changes the mix from one trail to the next. Check the park or forest page near you, or walk the loop you already use and see what has turned. A block with few trees still works. The door, the bike, and the window are on the list. If autumn where you live arrives in the other half of the year, save the prompts for autumn there.

:::bee
The [Forest Service](https://www.fs.usda.gov/visit/fall-colors/joy-and-science) notes that many native bees and butterflies rely on leaf litter to complete their life cycles. Leave a corner unraked. The photo of the pile can come from the sidewalk.
:::
