Walking indoors counts the same as walking outside. At a steady pace of about 100 steps a minute, ten minutes of laps is about **1,000** steps and a half hour is about **3,000**. Pick a place with room for a loop (a mall, a long hallway, a station concourse, your own flat), carry the thing that counts your steps, and keep moving.

## About 1,000 steps in 10 minutes

In [CADENCE-Adults](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6337834/), Tudor-Locke and colleagues set 100 steps a minute as a heuristic threshold for moderate-intensity walking in healthy adults ages 21 to 40. The paper calls these heuristic values "evidence-based, practical, rounded numbers." The [CADENCE-Adults paper on ages 61 to 85](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8461976/) found the same 100 steps a minute for moderate intensity, consistent with the authors' earlier findings in adults 21 to 40 and 41 to 60. Both were treadmill studies. Ages 21 to 40 were healthy adults. Ages 61 to 85 were ambulatory adults the authors called ostensibly healthy. Treat 100 steps a minute as a planning pace, and the totals below as estimates for a pace you hold with no long pauses.

At that steady pace, 10 × 100 = **1,000** steps, 20 × 100 = **2,000**, and 30 × 100 = **3,000**. Easy pottering, about 80 steps a minute, is 10 × 80 = **800** in ten minutes and 30 × 80 = **2,400** in a half hour.

| Pace | Per minute | In 10 minutes | In 30 minutes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | about 80 | about 800 | about 2,400 |
| Steady | about 100 | about 1,000 | about 3,000 |
| Brisk | about 120 | about 1,200 | about 3,600 |
| Very brisk | about 130 | about 1,300 | about 3,900 |

The easy, brisk, and very brisk rows are the same planning paces as [how many steps are in a 30-minute walk](/guides/how-many-steps-is-a-30-minute-walk), about 80, about 120, and about 130. A living-room loop with a turn at every doorway spends some of those minutes pivoting, so the count can come in a little under the table.

## Mall walking

Mall walking is organized enough to have its own guide. In 2015, Belza and colleagues at the University of Washington Health Promotion Research Center published [Mall Walking: A Program Resource Guide](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/31291/cdc_31291_DS1.pdf). The [UW School of Public Health](https://sph.washington.edu/news-events/news/sph-and-cdc-release-mall-walking-guide) says the CDC sponsored and co-authored it.

The release lists a climate-controlled indoor environment, mall security staff and other walkers and shoppers, level surfaces that can reduce the risk of slips and falls, social support from fellow mall walkers, and well-lit restrooms with access to a free water fountain. The guide itself says the majority of malls provide a temperature-controlled indoor environment regardless of the season, that security staff are usually present, and that malls have level surfaces, water fountains for free water, and accessible restrooms. A loop through a mall that is already open still counts.

## A hallway, a concourse, or the flat

A long hallway, or the corridors of a building you are already in, is the same loop with fewer shops. A station concourse or a big store works when you can keep moving and stay out of the way of people catching a train or buying something.

At home, walk a kitchen to hallway to living room circuit. Take a loop during a phone call, and another while the kettle boils. If you have stairs and they are comfortable for you, a flight can sit inside the same ten minutes.

## The phone on the counter

Indoors, the phone ends up on a counter. A phone only counts steps while you carry it. Pocket it for the laps, or wear the watch. A phone left on a desk misses the walk, as covered in [why phone and watch step counts don't match](/guides/why-phone-and-watch-step-counts-dont-match).

## The minutes still count

The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html) says adults need at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week, such as brisk walking. You can spread that time through the week and break it into smaller chunks. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/walking-for-health/) says a brisk 10-minute daily walk counts toward the 150 minutes of weekly exercise in the physical activity guidelines for adults aged 19 to 64. A brisk lap indoors is still brisk walking, and those minutes count toward the same 150.

## A cold-week rule for the hive

Indoor laps count. Pin one line where the group will see it. On a below-freezing day, 10 minutes inside counts. Post a photo of the loop, whether that is a mall atrium, a hallway, a stairwell, or the kettle.

A hive is a few friends, a daily step count, and one photo. That shape is [what a hive is](/guides/what-is-a-hive). The week-long version in a chat you already have is [how to start a walking streak with friends](/guides/start-a-walking-streak-with-friends).

If those indoor loops are feeding a 24-day group goal, see [a December walking challenge for friends](/guides/december-walking-challenge-for-friends).

When you do head out after dark, [walking after the clocks go back](/guides/walking-after-the-clocks-go-back) covers visibility and the ten-minute minimum.
