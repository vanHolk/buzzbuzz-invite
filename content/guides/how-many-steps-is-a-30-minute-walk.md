A 30-minute walk is about **3,000 to 4,000 steps**, depending on pace. Steady walking, about 100 steps a minute, is **3,000**, and a very brisk walk near 130 steps a minute is about **3,900**.

An easy stroll near 80 steps a minute is about **2,400** in the same half hour, and a brisk 120 is about **3,600**. Those are the planning cadences on the [10,000-step time guide](/guides/how-long-to-walk-10000-steps): about 80 slow, about 100 average, about 120 and up for brisk. Count the minutes you are walking. A red light, a shoelace, or a conversation on the corner keeps the clock running and adds no steps.

## A 30-minute pace chart

Multiply the cadence by 30. The rows assume you keep the pace, with no long pauses.

| Pace | Per minute | In 30 minutes | Distance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slow walk | about 80 | about 2,400 | about 1.0–1.2 miles |
| Average walk | about 100 | about 3,000 | about 1.2–1.5 miles |
| Brisk walk | about 120 | about 3,600 | about 1.4–1.8 miles |
| Very brisk walk | about 130 | about 3,900 | about 1.6–2.0 miles |
| Easy run | about 150–170 | about 4,500–5,100 | about 5 km in one lab sample |

[Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/walking-for-exercise) puts 30 minutes of walking at around 3,000 to 4,000 steps. [Tudor-Locke and colleagues, in a 2008 review](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2008/07002/revisiting__how_many_steps_are_enough__.5.aspx), tie that range to moderate intensity: a cadence of about 100 steps a minute or faster. 100 × 30 = 3,000, the floor of the band. 130 × 30 = 3,900, close to the 4,000 end. 120 × 30 = 3,600 sits in the middle, which is why a brisk walk on this page is still about 3,600. 80 × 30 = 2,400 stays under the band. That stroll is slower than the moderate floor.

In [CADENCE-Adults](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12966-019-0769-6) (Tudor-Locke and colleagues, 2019), healthy adults ages 21 to 40 walked on a treadmill. The practical thresholds were about 100 steps a minute for moderate intensity and about 130 for vigorous intensity. The same paper lined up about 120 steps a minute with a harder effort, about 5 METs, one notch below vigorous. The published thresholds were rounded from the lab figures, so treat them as reference points rather than a pace you need to hold. The 80 steps a minute row is the easy stroll shared with the time guide. It is not a threshold from that study.

Walking distance in the table is the step count divided by 2,000 to 2,500 steps in a mile, the range in the [1 mile guide](/guides/how-many-steps-is-1-mile). A shorter step covers less ground, so the same count finishes nearer the low end. 2,400 ÷ 2,500 = 0.96 miles and 2,400 ÷ 2,000 = 1.2 miles, shown as about 1.0–1.2. 3,000 ÷ 2,500 = 1.2 miles and 3,000 ÷ 2,000 = 1.5 miles. The brisk row is 3,600 ÷ 2,500 = 1.44 miles and 3,600 ÷ 2,000 = 1.8 miles, shown as about 1.4–1.8. The very brisk row is 3,900 ÷ 2,500 = 1.56 miles and 3,900 ÷ 2,000 = 1.95 miles, shown as about 1.6–2.0.

The [NHS describes a brisk walk as about 3 miles an hour](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/walking-for-health/), faster than a stroll, and you can still talk. Half an hour at that speed is **1.5 miles**, about **2.4 km**, inside the brisk distance. A mile and a half at 2,000 to 2,500 steps a mile is 1.5 × 2,000 = **3,000 steps** and 1.5 × 2,500 = **3,750 steps**. The brisk count, about 3,600, sits in that band. The minutes and the miles roughly agree.

The kilometre version lines up too. [5 km is roughly 6,500 steps](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km) for a lot of people, about 1,300 steps in a kilometre. At that figure, 2.4 km is 1,300 × 2.4 = **about 3,120 steps**, close to the steady row.

The run row is a different gait. A 2025 review describes typical recreational running cadence as about 150 to 170 steps a minute ([Figueiredo, Reis e Silva, and Sousa](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12440572/)). 150 × 30 = **4,500 steps**. 170 × 30 = **5,100**. Distance does not fall out of cadence alone. [Malisoux and colleagues (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10588426/) timed 860 recreational runners at the speed each person chose. The average speed was 9.9 km/h, about 10 km/h, and the average cadence was 164 steps a minute, inside that 150 to 170 range. Half an hour at 9.9 km/h is about **5 km** (about **3 miles**). A slower jog covers less. Their treadmill is not your route.

## How many minutes is 3,000 steps?

Turn the chart around. Divide 3,000 by the cadence.

- Slow, about 80 steps a minute: 3,000 ÷ 80 = **37.5 minutes**
- Average, about 100 steps a minute: 3,000 ÷ 100 = **30 minutes**
- Brisk, about 120 steps a minute: 3,000 ÷ 120 = **25 minutes**
- Very brisk, about 130 steps a minute: 3,000 ÷ 130 = **about 23 minutes**

An easy run in that 150 to 170 range is shorter on the clock. 3,000 ÷ 150 = **20 minutes**. 3,000 ÷ 170 = **about 18 minutes**. A red light still counts on the watch and not on the step total, so a real outing runs longer than the division.

## Check yours in 60 seconds

A minute of counting beats a stranger’s average.

1. Walk at the pace you mean to hold and count steps for 60 seconds. If a full minute makes you speed up or slow down, count for 15 seconds and multiply by 4.
2. Multiply that steps-per-minute number by 30.
3. The product is your 30-minute estimate, provided the pace stays put.

A count of 90 in that minute is 90 × 30 = **2,700 steps**. A count of 110 is 110 × 30 = **3,300 steps**.

Or note the counter, walk for 30 minutes, and subtract. If the clock says 40 and you were moving for 30, use 30. Next time, start from that number.

## Where a half hour fits

The [CDC asks adults for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html), such as brisk walking. You can spread that time through the week and break it into smaller chunks. The CDC's own example is 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week. Five 30-minute walks are 5 × 30 = **150 minutes**. Three 10-minute walks cover one of those days in smaller pieces.

On the step side, one steady half hour is about **3,000 steps**, and three of them are 3 × 3,000 = **about 9,000**. Walking between those loops, to the station or around the block, gets a lot of people the rest of the way toward 10,000. [Walking 10,000 steps usually takes about 90–120 minutes](/guides/how-long-to-walk-10000-steps) of actual walking. Three half hours are 90 minutes, the short end of that range. At 100 steps a minute, 10,000 steps takes 10,000 ÷ 100 = **100 minutes**, so the steps outside the three loops still matter.

## Why two counters disagree

Your counter tallies footfalls, not minutes, so the same half hour can land higher or lower for a few ordinary reasons:

- **Pace.** More steps a minute means more steps in the half hour.
- **Step length.** At the same speed, a shorter step needs a quicker cadence, so the count rises. A longer step does the reverse.
- **Stops and terrain.** Hills, crowds, and crossings spend minutes that never become steps.
- **The device.** A phone in a bag and a watch on a wrist are both guessing from movement. Different guesses are normal.

The longer version of the step-length math is in the [1 mile guide](/guides/how-many-steps-is-1-mile). For a timed walk, cadence and pauses do most of the shifting.
