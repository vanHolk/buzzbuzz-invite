The easiest Thanksgiving walk is 10 to 20 minutes, right after the leftovers go in the fridge, at the pace of the slowest person coming. That is roughly **1,000 to 2,000 steps** at a steady pace, short enough that the whole table can say yes, and you are back before pie.

## The fourth Thursday

Thanksgiving is the fourth Thursday in November. In 2026 it is Thursday, November 26, on the [OPM list of federal holidays](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/federal-holidays/).

## Fridge the leftovers, then walk

[USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety) says leftovers should be refrigerated within 2 hours. Perishable food left at room temperature for more than 2 hours should be thrown away, and the limit drops to 1 hour when the room is above 90°F. USDA also says to cut the turkey into smaller pieces to refrigerate. Slice the breast meat. Legs and wings can stay whole.

Clear the table, fill the containers, and shut the fridge. Then walk. A 20-minute loop fits easily once that door is closed. A two-hour wander with the turkey still on the counter does not.

## A short walk after the big meal

[Reynolds and colleagues (2016, Diabetologia)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00125-016-4085-2) ran a randomised crossover study with 41 adults who had type 2 diabetes. Advice to walk 10 minutes after each main meal produced lower post-meal blood sugar than one 30-minute walk a day, about **12%** lower overall. The biggest difference came after the evening meal, about **22%** lower, when people ate the most carbohydrate and sat the most.

[Buffey and colleagues (2022, Sports Medicine)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01649-4) pooled seven one-day lab trials, mostly in adults with overweight or obesity. Breaking up long sitting with short light-intensity walks lowered post-meal glucose and insulin compared with staying seated, and walking beat standing.

The walks in these studies were short and light. Nobody needs a power march after pie. These are small, specific studies, and anyone managing diabetes should follow their own care plan.

## Get a mixed group out the door

Name a time before the meal, while the table is still being set. "We walk at 4:30, pie after." A walk proposed from the couch loses.

The slowest walker sets the pace. Grandparents, a toddler, a stroller, and the cousin in dress shoes can all be that person. The faster cousins can take another lap once everyone is back at the door.

Pick a loop, not an out-and-back. Choose one that passes the house near the 10-minute mark, so anyone who is done can peel off at the door and the rest keep going.

Give the walk a small job. Grab something from the corner, check the neighbor's inflatable turkey, or find the best leaf on the block. A small errand gets coats on. "Let's get some exercise" starts a debate.

Late-November evenings get dark early in much of the US. If the time you named lands after sunset, [walking after the clocks go back](/guides/walking-after-the-clocks-go-back) covers being visible on that loop.

## Steps worth texting

At about 100 steps a minute, 10 minutes is about **1,000 steps**, 20 minutes is about **2,000 steps**, and 30 minutes is about **3,000 steps**. That pace is the estimate in [how many steps are in a 30-minute walk](/guides/how-many-steps-is-a-30-minute-walk). A stroller, a hill, or a pause to look at a yard decoration will move the real count. Treat the round numbers as an estimate.

A Thanksgiving-morning 5K, the turkey trot common in many US towns, is about **6,500 steps** if you walk it. That matches [how many steps are in 5 km](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km). The after-dinner loop is the outing between the fridge and the pie. The step count is a fun number to send, not a target.

## Friends at different tables

Friends scatter to their own families. Each person walks after their own dinner and sends one photo to the group chat: the weirdest yard decoration, the leftover-container count, or the dog in a sweater. Time zones mean the check-ins trickle in all evening.

That is a one-day [hive](/guides/what-is-a-hive): three to eight friends and one check-in. Separate dining rooms still count. Thursday through Sunday is four days, and four short walks make a tidy first streak. The week-long playbook, including a day that goes quiet, is [start a walking streak with friends](/guides/start-a-walking-streak-with-friends).

BuzzBuzz works for this. So does any group chat.
