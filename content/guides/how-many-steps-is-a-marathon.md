Walking a marathon is **about 56,000 steps** for many adults. Step length moves that count, and the usual walking band is **about 53,000–63,000**. A half marathon is **about 28,000**. Running the same road takes fewer, longer steps, roughly **36,000–43,000**.

Treat the range as a sketch. Then check it on a walk you take.

## The distance

[World Athletics](https://worldathletics.org/disciplines/road-running/marathon) sets the marathon at 26 miles 385 yards, standardised in 1921. That is **42.195 km**, or **42,195 metres**. A mile is 1,760 yards, and 385 ÷ 1,760 = 0.21875, so 26 miles 385 yards is 26.21875 miles, written as **26.2 miles**.

Half that distance is 42,195 ÷ 2 = 21,097.5 metres, which is **21.0975 km**. 26.2 ÷ 2 = **13.1 miles**.

## A step-length chart for the full and the half

Divide the metres by the length of one step. These rows use the same example lengths as the [10 km](/guides/how-many-steps-is-10km) and [5 miles](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5-miles) charts. They are examples, not a reading of your walk.

- About 0.67 m: 42,195 ÷ 0.67 = 62,978, roughly **63,000 steps**. The half is 21,097.5 ÷ 0.67 = 31,489, roughly **31,500 steps**.
- About 0.75 m: 42,195 ÷ 0.75 = 56,260, roughly **56,000 steps**. The half is 21,097.5 ÷ 0.75 = 28,130, roughly **28,000 steps**.
- About 0.80 m: 42,195 ÷ 0.80 = 52,744, roughly **53,000 steps**. The half is 21,097.5 ÷ 0.80 = 26,372, roughly **26,400 steps**.

The [University of Nebraska Medical Center’s walking guide](https://www.unmc.edu/cfhl/resources/walking.html) puts an average stride at about 2.5 feet and says a mile takes just over 2,000 steps. [How many steps are in 1 mile](/guides/how-many-steps-is-1-mile) works that 2.5 foot stride out as 5,280 ÷ 2.5 = 2,112 steps a mile. Across 26.2 miles, 26.2 × 2,112 = 55,334, about **55,000 steps**, close to the 0.75 m row.

## Running steps from one race

Cordingley, Leiter, and McRae filmed runners during a marathon and reported the step lengths in [Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1249/01.mss.0000675944.89026.97). At km 11 the average was about **1.16 m** for the men and **1.05 m** for the women. Near km 39 the steps were shorter, about **1.05 m** for the men and **0.98 m** for the women. These were runners filmed in one race.

The same 42,195 metres, divided by those lengths:

- 42,195 ÷ 1.16 = 36,375, about **36,400 steps**
- 42,195 ÷ 1.05 = 40,186, about **40,200 steps**
- 42,195 ÷ 0.98 = 43,056, about **43,100 steps**

Dividing the whole course by a length measured at one marker is an estimate, and tired legs late in the race take shorter steps. For runners like those, the course lands around **36,000–43,000 steps**, roughly 13,000–20,000 fewer than the 0.75 m walking row.

## How long the walk takes

Pace sets the clock. The [NHS describes a brisk walk as about 3 miles an hour](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/walking-for-health/), faster than a stroll, and you can still talk. At that pace, 26.2 ÷ 3 comes to **8 hours 44 minutes** of brisk walking. A half marathon at the same pace is 13.1 ÷ 3, which is **4 hours 22 minutes**.

Stops add clock time, and they add no steps.

## A marathon spread over ordinary days

Seven days at 8,000 steps is 7 × 8,000 = **56,000**, the round figure on the 0.75 m row. Ten days at 10,000 steps is 10 × 10,000 = 100,000. 100,000 ÷ 56,260 = 1.78, about **1.8** times that walking marathon, almost two. [How many miles is 10,000 steps](/guides/how-many-miles-is-10000-steps) and [how long 10,000 steps takes](/guides/how-long-to-walk-10000-steps) cover one of those days on its own.

## Six people, one day's steps

Six people sharing a marathon's worth of steps is 56,260 ÷ 6 = 9,377, about **9,400 steps** each. Eight people is 56,260 ÷ 8 = 7,032.5, about **7,000 steps** each. [A hive](/guides/what-is-a-hive) is a small group of friends who can share a day's steps.

:::bee
Nobody in the hive has to run anything. Six ordinary walking days, one marathon's worth of steps, and the bragging rights get split six ways.
:::

## Find your own marathon

Walk a mile you already know, a track lap or a route measured on a map. Note the counter at the start and at the end, subtract, and multiply by 26.2. For a half, multiply by 13.1. That product is your course. Your own number beats the chart.

On a steep or rocky trail, shorter steps raise the count for the same distance. [A hike still counts](/guides/does-a-hike-count-toward-your-step-goal), and a hilly course can take more steps than the pavement rows above.

Walk a share with a few friends this weekend. [Start a walking streak with friends](/guides/start-a-walking-streak-with-friends) if you want the same people out again tomorrow.
