10,000 steps is about 4 to 5 miles for most adults, or about 6.5 to 8 km. At a 2.5 foot step, the count is about 4.7 miles (7.6 km). Step length moves the distance. Treat the range as an estimate.

## Why the same count covers a different distance

A step counter counts footfalls. Distance is that count times the length of each step. A shorter step covers less ground, so 10,000 of them finish nearer 4 miles. A longer step finishes nearer 5 miles, and a very long one can finish past 5 miles.

Walking faster often stretches a step a little. Hills, sand, crowds, and a path full of turns are not a flat track. A phone in a bag and a watch on a wrist are both guessing from movement, so two totals on one walk can disagree. The figures below hold step length steady.

## 10,000 steps at three step lengths

A mile is 5,280 feet. [How many steps are in 1 mile](/guides/how-many-steps-is-1-mile) divides that by your step. For a count you already have, multiply.

**miles ≈ 10,000 × step length (ft) ÷ 5,280**

Kilometres use 0.3048 metres in a foot. These rows are examples, not a measurement of your walk.

- 2.1 ft step → 21,000 ft → 4.0 miles (6.4 km)
- 2.5 ft step → 25,000 ft → 4.7 miles (7.6 km)
- 2.8 ft step → 28,000 ft → 5.3 miles (8.5 km)

The middle row works out as 10,000 × 2.5 = 25,000 feet, then 25,000 ÷ 5,280 = 4.73 miles, which rounds to 4.7. And 25,000 × 0.3048 = 7.62 km, which rounds to 7.6. The same division gives the short row (21,000 ÷ 5,280 = 3.98 miles, shown as 4.0) and the long row (28,000 ÷ 5,280 = 5.30 miles, shown as 5.3).

The [University of Nebraska Medical Center’s walking guide](https://www.unmc.edu/cfhl/resources/walking.html) says the average person’s stride is about 2.5 feet, a mile takes just over 2,000 steps, and 10,000 steps is close to five miles. Divide 5,280 by 2.5 and you get 2,112 steps in a mile. Divide 10,000 by 2,112 and you get 4.7 miles again. Their 2.5 feet is one footfall. This page calls that a step.

Step length is the distance from one foot’s contact to the other foot’s contact. Stride length is the distance from one contact of a foot back to the next contact of that same foot. The [University of Oklahoma Health Sciences Center](https://ouhsc.edu/bserdac/dthompso/web/gait/KNMATICS/stride.htm) defines them that way. For the men, that table lists a 79 cm step and a 158 cm stride. For the women, it lists a 66 cm step and a 132 cm stride. The page credits Murray, Drought, and Kory (1964) for the men and Murray, Kory, and Sepic (1970) for the women. A 79 cm step times 10,000 is 7,900 metres, or 7.9 km. 7,900 ÷ 0.3048 ÷ 5,280 comes out to 4.9 miles. A 66 cm step times 10,000 is 6,600 metres, or 6.6 km, and the same division comes out to 4.1 miles. Both sit inside 4 to 5 miles. They are means from those studies, not a reading of your walk.

About 4 to 5 miles fits a step of about 2.1 to 2.6 feet. Four miles is 6.4 km and five miles is 8.0 km, so the round kilometre version of that band is about 6.5 to 8. The 2.1 foot row is 6.4 km, on the short side of that rounding. The 2.8 foot row is 5.3 miles and 8.5 km, outside the band. The step count for that five-mile end is [how many steps is 5 miles](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5-miles).

The rows above are walking steps. A running step is often longer, so the same 10,000 can pass 5 miles. [Runner’s World](https://www.runnersworld.com/training/a60492650/how-many-miles-is-10000-steps/) reports exercise physiologist Heather Hart’s view that a run usually covers more miles in 10,000 steps than a walk, unless each step is very short and the cadence is high. Check a run the same way as a walk. Divide the distance by the steps.

[Mayo Clinic Press](https://mcpress.mayoclinic.org/nutrition-fitness/does-hitting-10000-steps-a-day-really-make-a-difference/) prints a simpler chart: every 1,000 steps is about half a mile and about 10 minutes, so 10,000 steps is 5 miles and 100 minutes. Half a mile in 10 minutes is 3 miles an hour, and 1,000 steps in 10 minutes is 100 steps a minute. A mile at 2,000 steps is 5,280 ÷ 2,000 = 2.64 feet per step, a little longer than the 2.5 foot row. That is why this page gets 4.7 miles there, and 5 miles only as the long end of the range. The minutes are a separate question. [How long 10,000 steps takes](/guides/how-long-to-walk-10000-steps) keeps the clock.

## Other daily totals at a 2.5 foot step

Same step, same formula, still estimates. Feet are the step count times 2.5. Miles are those feet divided by 5,280.

- 5,000 steps → 12,500 ft → 2.4 miles (3.8 km)
- 7,500 steps → 18,750 ft → 3.6 miles (5.7 km)
- 10,000 steps → 25,000 ft → 4.7 miles (7.6 km)
- 12,000 steps → 30,000 ft → 5.7 miles (9.1 km)
- 15,000 steps → 37,500 ft → 7.1 miles (11.4 km)

18,750 ÷ 5,280 = 3.55 miles, shown as 3.6, and 18,750 × 0.3048 = 5.72 km, shown as 5.7. 37,500 ÷ 5,280 = 7.10 miles, shown as 7.1, and 37,500 × 0.3048 = 11.43 km, shown as 11.4. A shorter step than 2.5 feet shrinks every row. A longer step grows them.

[5 km is roughly 6,500 steps](/guides/how-many-steps-is-5km) for a lot of people. At this 2.5 foot step, 7,500 steps is about 5.7 km, a little past that 5 km. [10 km is about 13,000 steps](/guides/how-many-steps-is-10km) for many adults, so 10,000 steps usually stops short of a 10 km.

## Measure your own

Walk a distance you know, then divide that distance by the steps you took.

1. Pick a route you know is about a mile or a kilometre, or measure one on a map before you leave.
2. Note the step count at the start and at the end.
3. Subtract. That gap is your steps for the route.
4. Divide the distance by the gap to get your step length, then multiply by 10,000.

Use that length next time, instead of a stranger’s average.

An Apple Watch can learn your stride on a calibration walk. On the paired iPhone, turn on Location Services and Motion Calibration & Distance. Wear the watch somewhere flat and open with clear GPS, open the Workout app, tap Outdoor Walk, and walk at your normal pace for about 20 minutes. Apple says you can split those 20 minutes across outdoor sessions. Ultra models, Series 8 and later, and SE (2nd generation) use the watch’s GPS even with the iPhone nearby. Older models use the iPhone’s GPS when it is available. The menu path is on [Calibrate your Apple Watch](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105048).

## Where 10,000 came from

Tudor-Locke and Bassett, writing in Sports Medicine in 2004, trace the 10,000-step target to Japanese walking clubs and a 1960s slogan from the pedometer maker Yamasa. They report that Dr Yoshiro Hatano described a pedometer reaching the Japanese market in 1965 under the name manpo-kei, translated as a ten-thousand-steps meter.

## How long those steps take

[Walking 10,000 steps usually takes about 90–120 minutes](/guides/how-long-to-walk-10000-steps) of actual walking.
